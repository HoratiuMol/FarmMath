River & woods backdrop, refined

Second pass on option C (rounded/painterly), addressing "doesn't feel part of the same map yet": the river now visibly starts and ends in the terrain instead of floating over blank ground, and the woods borrow the same visual grammar — ink outlines, a warm highlight fleck, a soft ground-contact shadow — already used by the cow and barn, instead of being flat shapes dropped on top.

What changed from the first pass, and why:
  • Source: the river used to just begin mid-air near the top edge. It now bubbles up from a small rock spring (ink-outlined stones, same brown family as the fence posts) with a couple of soft mist puffs drifting off it, and fades in from zero width over its first stretch — reads as "emerging from the terrain," not "a shape that starts at pixel X."
  • Mouth & woods placement: the tree cluster used to be positioned independently of the river, so the two never quite touched. The woods anchor is now derived directly from the river path's own endpoint, and the river widens into a small pool tucked under the first (largest) tree's canopy, with lily pads and a couple of rocks — the river visibly flows into the woods rather than stopping beside them.
  • Grounding shadows: every tree and the pool now get the same soft dark radial-falloff contact shadow the barn uses (drawBarn's shadow gradient) — this was most of what made the first pass read as "pasted on" rather than sitting on the ground.
  • Ink outlines + palette: tree canopies, trunks, rocks and reeds now get the cow's dark ink stroke (#3a332b) around every shape, and canopy greens were pulled from the wheat/carrot blade palette already in the game instead of an unrelated green — same "family" of colors and line-work as everything else on screen.
  • Banks: a few reed tufts (wheat/carrot-green blades) line the banks at intervals, echoing the crop tiles' own blade-scatter technique instead of a bare shoreline.
